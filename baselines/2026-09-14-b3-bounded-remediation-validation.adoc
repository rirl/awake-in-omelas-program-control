== Correctability of Admitted Artifacts

B3 exposed a useful governance distinction between historical immutability and file-level correctability.

The following program-control principle is adopted upon admission of this record:

[quote]
____
Admitted artifacts are not silently mutable, but they are correctable through a new governed admission event.
____

Admission makes the historical version of an artifact immutable in the admitted Git history. It does not require the current working-tree representation of that artifact to remain permanently uncorrected.

An admitted artifact may be corrected when:

* the original admitted version remains preserved in Git history;
* the correction is explicit and narrowly scoped;
* the correction does not silently alter evidentiary or semantic meaning;
* the correction is reviewed and validated according to its risk;
* the change is committed as a new historical event rather than rewriting prior history; and
* new evidence records what was corrected, why it was corrected, how it was validated, and how the corrected version relates to the original admitted version.

Substantive semantic corrections require stronger review and admission than purely mechanical remediation and must not be represented as formatting-only changes.

This principle is currently a program-control rule derived from empirical B3 experience. It should be evaluated during architectural separation for possible promotion into reusable KIOS governance.
