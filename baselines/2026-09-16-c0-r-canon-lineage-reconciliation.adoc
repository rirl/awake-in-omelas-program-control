== Semantic Reconciliation Findings

Read-only semantic comparison of the four identified canon slices established a
consistent distinction between historical path absence and semantic absence.

For C0-R purposes, representation is evaluated using the following categories:

* *Path absence* — the stranded artifact is absent at its historical path in the
  controlled baseline.
* *Concept reference* — the baseline names, anticipates, schedules, or otherwise
  references the concept without materially defining it.
* *Partial semantic representation* — meaningful elements of the concept exist in
  the baseline, but significant narrative, provenance, causal, relational,
  symbolic, or constraint information remains absent.
* *Substantive semantic absence* — the stranded lineage contains material semantic
  structure not represented in the controlled baseline.

All 27 stranded canon/provenance files examined are absent at their exact historical
paths in the controlled baseline.

Semantic-anchor comparison established that exact path absence does not by itself
determine whether the associated canon meaning is absent.

The current slice-level findings are:

=== Abramson / Photograph / Family Memory

The controlled baseline references Shlomo Abramson, Shmuel Abramson, and a
photograph-related question, but does not contain the detailed semantic slice.

The stranded lineage contributes substantive canon concerning Professor Shlomo
Abramson, Shmuel Abramson's historical role, the old family photograph as a
provenance anchor, memory-through-image narrative structure, the photograph
provenance chain, family-photograph symbolism, and associated chronology.

Current reconciliation constraints include:

* Shmuel Abramson is not established as Shlomo Abramson's father.
* Shmuel Abramson is not established as "Shlomo Abramson Sr."
* Eliezer Abramson is not established as directly related to Shlomo Abramson.
* the photograph is the narrative and provenance linkage among these materials;
* chronology remains partially unresolved.

Current finding: *substantive canon expansion rather than duplication*.

The controlled baseline references ATON-V8 for future introduction or population,
but detailed ATON canon is absent.

=== Architectural Validation Finding

The canon structure supports semantic reconciliation across divergent history.

Git identifies divergent history, commits, paths, and blobs.

Semantic anchors provide a mechanism for locating the same concept across divergent
history.

Structured canon metadata and sections provide a mechanism for understanding and
comparing the concept's meaning, provenance, relationships, chronology,
constraints, and narrative function.

The reconciliation method can therefore be summarized as:

----

      |
      v

      |
      v

      |
      v

      |
      v

      |
      v

These are reconciliation findings only.

They do not constitute recovery dispositions, canon admission, authorization to
restore historical paths, or authorization to begin C1.

== C0-R Governance Constraints

The following controls apply during C0-R:

* wholesale merge of `feature/initial-canon-entries` is prohibited;
* rebase of the stranded lineage into the current architecture branch is prohibited;
* indiscriminate cherry-picking of the stranded lineage is prohibited;
* selective reconciliation is required;
* reconciliation must remain reviewable;
* provenance must be preserved;

  responsibility rather than historical co-location;
* no tooling-retention decision may be inferred merely from preserving the recovery
  lineage;
* no architectural extraction begins as part of C0-R.

== Selective Reconciliation Model

C0-R governs reconciliation by semantic responsibility rather than by historical
branch membership.

The working model is:

----
stranded lineage
      |
      +-- canon content + provenance
      |
      +-- bootstrap / framework material
      |
      +-- historical generation tooling
----

Each class is evaluated independently.

Historical co-location in Git is evidence of lineage, not proof of future
architectural ownership.
